Digital publishing is handled as a single pipeline from manuscript to
distribution — editing and layout decisions are made with the final
distributed format in mind from the start.

## Editing

Manuscript editing covers developmental structure, line-level clarity and
copyediting, done as separate passes rather than one rushed read-through.
A manuscript that will be self-published needs a different level of
editorial rigor than one going to a traditional publisher's own editorial
team, and the scope is agreed up front based on which situation applies.

## Layout and typesetting

Layout decisions — trim size, typeface, margins, chapter openers, running
heads — are made with the eBook's actual reading environment in mind:
reflowable text on e-readers and phones behaves very differently from a
fixed print layout, so typesetting is done for the format the reader will
actually use, not adapted after the fact from a print file. Where a
print edition is also wanted, that's scoped as a related but distinct
deliverable, since print and reflowable eBook layout follow different
rules.

## Cover design

Cover design is built to work at the sizes it will actually be seen at:
a thumbnail in a store listing and search results, not just a full-size
mockup. That means legible titling at small sizes, a composition that
still reads clearly in grayscale or low-light displays, and genre-aware
visual conventions so the cover signals the right category to a browsing
reader.

## Distribution prep

Each retailer and platform (Amazon KDP, Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play
Books, and others) has its own file format and metadata requirements.
Distribution prep means producing correctly formatted files for the
platforms actually being used, along with the metadata (categories,
keywords, description) that affects discoverability on each one —
handled once as part of the same pipeline rather than as a separate
project after the eBook is already finished.
